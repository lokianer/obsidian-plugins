Roadmap

Plugin 01 · dnd5e-byLokianer

Your campaign already knows who hates whom. Now you see it.

Factions are folders, relations are numbers you type, NPCs carry their stat blocks — and the plugin turns those plain Markdown notes into a living world you can read at a glance and change in place.

Module 1 · Factions · accepted

Factions are folders. Relations are numbers.

A folder with a factions.md is a faction; the character notes inside are its members; a faction folder inside another is a sub-faction. Each relation is a value from −5 war to +5 sworn — one-directional, with an optional reason. 

type: faction
power: 3
relations:            # the public face
  Order of the Lantern: -4
  The Archivists:
    value: 1
    why: "[[Mira Voss]]"
secret_relations:     # the true stance, GM only
  The Archivists: -2
  • Public and true. Secrets never show in anything marked public.
  • Inheritance. Members take their faction's stance unless they state their own.
  • Never fatal. Broken values become warnings with a one-click Fix, not a blank view.
  • A living map. Every faction in its own colour, on Obsidian's canvas dots; hover one and its stances light up with their values.

Module 2 · NPC builder

Stat blocks where you need them.

A dnd5e-statblock block holds a 5e stat block in the YAML Fantasy Statblocks users already know. The faction card puts AC, HP, CR, speed and the ability modifiers right under the name; the full block waits, folded, where it stands in the note — modifiers, XP and proficiency bonus worked out.

  • All 21 SRD 5.1 NPCs built in, checked against the SRD in tests.
  • New NPC. A dialog files the note into its faction: race, class, archetype, name, looks, voice, secret and hook — rolled from dice tables in English or German.
  • Your own. Archetypes, races and dice tables as plain notes; values edited right in the rendered block.
  • Compatible. One command converts Fantasy Statblocks blocks and back.

Module 2 · New NPC

Roll a whole person, keep what you like.

Pick a race, a class and an archetype from tiles — the SRD's nine races, twelve classes and twenty-one NPCs, plus your own as plain notes. Roll everything fills the rest; lock what you want to keep and roll again. The preview card shows the NPC as it will be written, stat block numbers included.

  • Your words win. Typing into a field locks it.
  • Bilingual dice. Every table has at least fifty entries, in English and German; German distances in metres.
  • Into the stat block. A race writes its SRD traits — size, speed, darkvision, languages, resistances.

Module 3 · Spellbook

Every SRD spell, one search away.

The 319 spells of the SRD 5.1 in their own tab: search in English or German, narrow by level and school chips, class, casting time, damage, save, concentration and ritual. Each spell wears its school's colour; the card shows it in both languages.

  • German too. Every spell's full text in German, distances in metres.
  • Keyboard first. ↑ ↓ through the list, Enter to read.
  • Linked. Spell names in a stat block open their card; your own spells join the list.
  • Offline. Everything ships with the plugin; no lookups on the web.

Module 4 · Deck of Many Things

Declare, draw, and let the rules run.

The SRD's Deck of Many Things on the table: name who draws, declare the cards, turn them one by one. The Fool and the Jester leave the deck, The Void and Donjon end the drawing, the Jester and the Idiot offer more draws — the tab keeps count.

  • A note keeps the deck. Ticked cards are out, every draw is a line.
  • 22 or 13 cards. The full deck, or the one most parties find.
  • Your own decks. Typed in, copied from the standard deck, pasted or imported from JSON.
  • German too. Wesir, Thron, Die Leere — every effect in German.

The session note is the spine

Change a stance, log it with one click.

After every change a notice offers Log → Session 14. One click writes a line under ## Log of tonight's session — secret changes inside a [!gm] callout — and the views show each relation's history in their tooltips. Starting the next session carries the loot over, each item marked with where it was found, so there is only ever one note to watch. 

## Log
- 20:31 Order of the Lantern → The Gutter Court: −2 → −3
- 21:40 The party now knows [[Lady Vermillion]].
> [!gm]
> - 21:52 The Bench → Order of the Lantern: −4 → +4

Try it on Silverstadt

The repository ships an example campaign — a city of tides, bells and records with five factions, secret stances, stat blocks and session notes. Build the plugin into it and open the folder as a vault. 

git clone https://github.com/lokianer/obsidian-plugins
cd obsidian-plugins
pnpm install
pnpm --filter dnd5e-bylokianer build
pnpm --filter dnd5e-bylokianer install-example
# open examples/vault in Obsidian, enable dnd5e-byLokianer

How the collection is built

Designed first, built in phases, checked in Obsidian.

Each plugin starts as a short design, then grows in phases with a plan and a review stop. Pure logic sits in tested TypeScript, the Obsidian API behind thin adapters, the views tested in a headless DOM. CI runs format, lint, strict typecheck, tests and build on Linux, macOS and Windows for every push. 

obsidian-plugins/
├── plugins/
│   └── dnd5e-bylokianer/  # one plugin per folder
├── packages/
│   └── session/           # shared, pure TypeScript
├── examples/vault/        # one test vault for all
├── docs/superpowers/      # designs and plans
