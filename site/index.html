Roadmap

Plugin 01 · dnd5e-byLokianer

Your campaign already knows who hates whom. Now you see it.

Factions are folders, relations are numbers you type, NPCs carry their stat blocks — and the plugin turns those plain Markdown notes into a living world you can read at a glance and change in place.

Module 1 · Factions · accepted

Factions are folders. Relations are numbers.

A folder with a factions.md is a faction; the character notes inside are its members; a faction folder inside another is a sub-faction. Each relation is a value from −5 war to +5 sworn — one-directional, with an optional reason. 

type: faction
power: 3
relations:            # the public face
  Order of the Lantern: -4
  The Archivists:
    value: 1
    why: "[[Mira Voss]]"
secret_relations:     # the true stance, GM only
  The Archivists: -2
  • Public and true. Secrets never show in anything marked public.
  • Inheritance. Members take their faction’s stance unless they state their own.
  • Never fatal. Broken values become warnings with a one-click Fix, not a blank view.
  • A living map. Every faction in its own colour, on Obsidian’s canvas dots; hover one and its stances light up with their values.

Module 2 · NPC builder · accepted

Stat blocks where you need them.

A dnd5e-statblock block holds a 5e stat block in the YAML Fantasy Statblocks users already know. The faction card puts AC, HP, CR, speed and the ability modifiers right under the name; the full block waits, folded, where it stands in the note — modifiers, XP and proficiency bonus worked out.

  • All 21 SRD 5.1 NPCs built in, checked against the SRD in tests.
  • Your own. Archetypes, races and dice tables as plain notes; values edited right in the rendered block.
  • Linked spells. Spell names in a Spellcasting trait open the spellbook.
  • Compatible. One command converts Fantasy Statblocks blocks and back.

Module 2 · New NPC

Roll a whole person, keep what you like.

Pick a race, a class and an archetype from tiles — the SRD’s 9 races, 12 classes and 21 NPCs, plus your own as plain notes. Roll everything fills the rest; lock what you want to keep and roll again. The preview card shows the NPC as it will be written, stat block numbers included.

  • Your words win. Typing into a field locks it.
  • Bilingual dice. Every table has at least 50 entries, in English and German; German distances in metres.
  • Into the stat block. A race writes its SRD traits — size, speed, darkvision, languages, resistances.

Module 3 · Spellbook · accepted

Every SRD spell, one search away.

The 319 spells of the SRD 5.1 in their own tab: search in English or German, narrow by level and school chips, class, casting time, damage, save, concentration and ritual. Each spell wears its school’s colour; the card shows it in both languages.

  • German too. Every spell’s full text in German, distances in metres.
  • Keyboard first. ↑ ↓ through the list, Enter to read.
  • Your own spells. type: spell notes join the list — or let the spell builder write one.
  • Offline. Everything ships with the plugin; no lookups on the web.

Spellbook · New spell

Start from any spell, make it yours.

The spell builder looks like New NPC: pick one of the 319 SRD spells from tiles and its words arrive in your language, ready to change — or fill every field by hand, or load a JSON file. The preview shows the card as the spellbook will.

  • School colours. Level and school as chips and tiles; the preview follows.
  • JSON in. Our own keys, or a spell in the 5e-bits or Open5e format.
  • A plain note out. Readable without the plugin, editable any time.

Module 4 · Deck of Many Things · accepted

Declare, draw, and let the rules run.

The SRD’s Deck of Many Things on the table: name who draws, declare the cards, turn them one by one. The Fool and the Jester leave the deck, The Void and Donjon end the drawing, the Jester and the Idiot offer more draws — the tab keeps count.

  • A note keeps the deck. Ticked cards are out, every draw is a line.
  • 22 or 13 cards. The full deck, or the one most parties find.
  • Your own decks. Typed in, copied from the standard deck, pasted or imported from JSON — then drawn in the same tab.
  • German too. Wesir, Thron, Die Leere — every effect in German.

Module 5 · Monster Manual · accepted

334 monsters, one search away.

The SRD’s bestiary in a tab built like the spellbook: search in English or German, narrow by challenge rating, type, size, movement, legendary and spellcaster. The card is the full stat block, in the type’s colour, with the spells of a Spellcasting trait linked.

  • Into your notes. Insert the stat block below your cursor, or make it a note.
  • German too. Names, sizes, types, metres — and every trait and action: “Nahkampf-Waffenangriff: +7 auf Treffer”.
  • Your own. A monster builder like New NPC — start from any SRD monster, or import from Fantasy Statblocks, 5etools or Open5e files.
  • One look. The same stat block view as in your notes.

Module 6 · Battle tracker · accepted

The fight lives in the encounter note.

An encounter note lists who fights — characters, notes, monsters from the Monster Manual, with a count. The Battle tab rolls initiative (one roll per group), runs the turns, tracks hit points, temporary hit points, death saves, conditions with their rounds and concentration — and writes it all to a plain ## Combat table in the note, so the fight survives a restart and reads fine without the plugin.

  • An encounter builder. Pick the party and monsters; the SRD 5.2.1’s XP budget says Low, Moderate or High.
  • Logged, not narrated. Begin, defeated, down, dead and the end go to tonight’s session; experience points only if you want them.
  • Mid-fight changes. Add to combat from the Monster Manual, or a name typed in.

Module 7 · Dice · accepted

Every roll gets its moment.

A dice stage modelled on Baldur’s Gate 3’s checks, drawn by the plugin itself: the difficulty class in its diamond, each die in its own shape tumbling and landing, the bonuses flying in, the total counting up, then Success or Failure — a natural 20 with golden rays. A click or Space skips straight to the result.

  • Formulas. 2d6+3, 4d6kh3, d20+5 adv, German 2W6+3.
  • In your notes. dice: 2d6+3 — Dice Roller’s syntax — and dice: [[Rumours#Night (d20)]] for a table.
  • Rollable tables. A heading ending in a die makes the list or table below it rollable; the row shows with its links.
  • Stat blocks and the party. Every +7 and 10 (1d10 + 5) is a button; characters roll their saves and skills.

Dice in every stat block

Click the +7. Shift for advantage.

In your notes, the Monster Manual and the Battle tab, attack bonuses, damage and every save and skill are buttons. A click plays the stage in a small window; Shift rolls with advantage, Alt with disadvantage, a long press offers both on a phone. Saving throw DCs stay text — the target rolls those. 

## Log
- 21:14 Owlbear rolls Beak: d20+7 → 19 (12, +7)
- 21:14 Owlbear rolls Beak damage: 1d10+5 → 11
- 21:15 Orrin Quill rolls Persuasion:
  2d20kh1+7 → 24 against DC 15: success

The session note is the spine

Change a stance, log it with one click.

After every change a notice offers Log, naming Session 14. One click writes a line under ## Log of tonight’s session — secret changes inside a [!gm] callout — and the views show each relation’s history in their tooltips. Starting the next session carries the loot over, each item marked with where it was found. 

## Log
- 20:31 Order of the Lantern → The Gutter Court: −2 → −3
- 21:40 The party now knows [[Lady Vermillion]].
> [!gm]
> - 21:52 The Bench → Order of the Lantern: −4 → +4

Try it on Silverstadt

The repository ships an example campaign — a city of tides, bells and records with five factions, secret stances, stat blocks, own spells and decks, and session notes. Build the plugin into it and open the folder as a vault — or, for your own vault, unpack the 0.1.0 ZIP into .obsidian/plugins/ and enable it. 

git clone https://github.com/lokianer/obsidian-plugins
cd obsidian-plugins
pnpm install
pnpm --filter dnd5e-bylokianer build
pnpm --filter dnd5e-bylokianer install-example
# open examples/vault in Obsidian, enable dnd5e-byLokianer

How the collection is built

Designed first, built in phases, checked in Obsidian.

Each plugin starts as a short design, then grows in phases with a plan and a review stop. Pure logic sits in tested TypeScript, the Obsidian API behind thin adapters, the views tested in a headless DOM. CI runs format, lint, strict typecheck, tests and build on Linux, macOS and Windows for every push.
